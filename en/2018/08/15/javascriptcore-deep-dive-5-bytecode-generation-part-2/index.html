StatementNode

StatementNode mainly describes language structures:

  1. Sequential structures: ClassDeclNode, DeclarationStateMentNode, and so on.
  2. Conditional structures: IFElseNode, SwitchNode, and so on.
  3. Loop structures: WhileNode, ForNode, DoWhileNode, and so on.

Next, I will pick several representative nodes and analyze how their bytecode is generated.

Backpatching

Unlike sequential structures, conditional and loop structures do not directly produce concrete logical instructions. They mainly generate jump instructions to control program flow.

When a jump instruction is generated, its destination may not be known immediately. So the target label is left unspecified for the moment. Once the correct target label can be determined, the instruction is patched with that target label. This technique is called backpatching.

You can also see that scopes are popped. This is easy to understand: break and continue are only valid in specific contexts, such as loop structures and switch structures. Once either is triggered, execution may leave the current switch or loop scope directly.
